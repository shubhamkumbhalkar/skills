# Humanizer

## Overview

AI-written prose has tells: em dashes, words like "leverage" and "robust", stacked hedges,
and smooth filler that says little. This skill rewrites or drafts text so it reads like a
careful human wrote it. Short sentences, plain words, real facts, no buzzwords.

## Usage

Use this skill when:
- Drafting or editing anything a person will read or receive: emails, cover letters, job or
  visa or media outreach, speaker bios, LinkedIn posts, proposals, documentation.
- The user says "humanize this", "make this sound human", or "strip the AI tells".

Do NOT use for:
- Code, logs, terse status lines, or machine output.

## Process

1. Read the draft (or the request) and identify the one point each sentence makes.
2. Rewrite to the rules below, one sentence at a time.
3. Run the final checks. If any fail, the draft is not done.

## Write like this
- Sentences average 10 to 20 words, one idea each, with the occasional longer one.
- Active voice at least 90 percent of the time. Everyday, concrete words over abstraction.
- Give numbers, names, and measurable facts. Connect ideas with plain words: and, but, so, then.
- Mix short and medium sentences. Vary paragraph length. State things directly. Do not hedge.

## Never
- No em dashes or en dashes. No semicolons. Use periods and commas.
- Never use these, in any form or capitalization: leverage, robust, cutting-edge, seamless,
  delve, foster, ensure, vital, essential, landscape (as metaphor), realm, testament, tapestry,
  navigating, elevate, embark, unleash, revolutionize, game-changer, paradigm, synergy,
  holistic, optimize, moreover, furthermore, however, therefore, additionally, consequently,
  ultimately, essentially, notably, firstly, in conclusion, in summary, it's worth noting,
  it's important to note.
- No cliches. No journey, music, or landscape metaphors. No corporate jargon. Never reference
  being an AI.

## Checks before returning the text
- Zero em or en dashes.
- Zero banned words.
- Short, varied sentences. Specific facts, not filler.
- Reads the way the user would actually say it out loud.
