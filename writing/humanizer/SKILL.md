# Humanizer

Apply when drafting or editing any prose the user will send or publish: emails, cover
letters, job / EB-1A / CACM / media outreach, CFPs, docs, posts, bios. Also apply on request
("humanize this"). Do NOT apply to routine status replies, code, logs, or tool output.

## Preserve the author's voice (do this first)
Change the tells, not the personality. Keep the author's own words, rhythm, humor, and quirks.
A humanized draft should still sound like the person who wrote it, with the AI residue gone.
If removing a tell would flatten a real voice, find a plainer phrasing that keeps the voice.

## Example
Before: "I'm thrilled to announce our cutting-edge platform will revolutionize how teams
collaborate. It's not just a tool, it's a paradigm shift."
After: "Our new platform is live. Two people can edit the same doc at once, and each change
shows up live for the other."

## Plain style
- Sentences 10 to 20 words, one idea each, mostly one verb phrase. Active voice around 90%.
  Everyday concrete words, real numbers, dates, names.
- Punctuation: periods, commas, question marks, occasional colons. No semicolons. No em dashes
  or en dashes.
- Connectors: and, but, so, then. At most one real question per 300 words, answered right away.
  A closing ask to the recipient is fine.
- Minimize modals (might, could, would, may, tends to) and hedging. No apologies, no cliches,
  no journey / music / landscape metaphors, no ALL-CAPS, no numbered headings unless an outline
  is asked for, no list items starting with transition words.
- Avoid the nouns: insight, perspective, solution, approach.

## Use sparingly (reach for a plainer option, but do not contort a sentence to avoid them)
These are normal English and real people use them. Prefer a simpler word first, keep them when
the alternative is more awkward: because, although, while, unless, even if, even though, despite,
in contrast, due to, in order to, given that, also, specifically, generally, similarly, as well
as, subsequently; nouns key, valuable, crucial; verbs ensure, enable, highlight; adverbs
quietly, actually, essentially, arguably, enduring. Minimize stacked hedges and modals.

## Banned phrases
I apologize for any confusion; I hope this helps; Please let me know if you need further
clarification; One might argue; Both sides have merit; In other words; Unlock the secrets of;
I hope this email finds you well; Thank you for reaching out; If you have any other questions,
feel free to ask.

## Structural AI tells to cut (remove on sight; *combo* = act only when several co-occur)
- No "not X but Y" framing, or any contrast that denies a claim nobody made.
- No dramatic one-line closers or emphatic fragments that just restate the point.
- No fake-profound endings or aphorisms dressed as hidden truths. State the plain point.
- No staged run-ups or throat-clearing openers ("let's dive in", "here's the thing", "let me be
  clear", "what nobody tells you").
- No inventing objections or rejected options that never came up.
- No forced lists of three. No colon reveals ("The best part: it learns").
- No inflated significance (turning point, bright future, stock challenges/outlook wrap-ups).
- No vague links ("associated with", "linked to", "tied to") without naming the relationship.
- No bolted-on "-ing" riders ("highlighting", "symbolizing", "fostering").
- No sales tone. No borrowed authority ("experts agree", "studies show") without a named source.
- Prefer "is/are/has" over "serves as", "features", "boasts". Name a thing the same way each
  time; no synonym cycling.
- No decorative bold, no title-case / emoji / arrow headings.
- No chatbot residue (greetings, praise, "great question", offers to help), no training-cutoff
  disclaimers, no invented gap-fills.
- Don't restate a heading in its first sentence. Don't narrate how the text was built. Don't
  re-explain what the reader already knows.
- Soft preference: avoid two consecutive sentences opening with the same subject.
- *combo-only:* stacked hedges, hyphenated pairs everywhere, passive voice / missing subjects,
  curly quotes.

## Exemptions (do not touch)
Quotations, titles, proper names, a phrase being discussed as a phrase, email salutations and
sign-offs.

## Workflow
Draft, then run one self-critique pass: mark each remaining tell, rewrite it, finalize. Preserve
voice throughout. Never invent facts to smooth prose; if a detail is missing, ask. Detect-only
mode is fine on request: quote each tell without rewriting.

Final check before returning: zero em/en dashes, zero hard-banned words, short varied sentences,
specific facts, and it still sounds like the author.
